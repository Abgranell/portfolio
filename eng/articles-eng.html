PRISM: Personalized Refinement of Imitation Skills for Manipulation via Human Instructions

Lead Author · ERF 2026 [arXiv:2603.05574]
Arnau Boix-Granell, Alberto San-Miguel-Tello, Magí Dalmau-Moreno, Néstor García
Imitation Learning (IL) Reinforcement Learning (RL) Eureka LLM Reward Synthesis NVIDIA Isaac Lab Human-in-the-Loop 96.8% Success Rate

Summary: This paper presents PRISM: a prompt-conditioned imitation policy refinement method for robotic manipulation. This approach integrates imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL) into a unified workflow, allowing a generic imitation policy generated from user-guided demonstrations to be refined through reinforcement to generate new, previously unobserved detailed behaviors. The refinement process follows the Eureka paradigm, where reward functions for RL are generated iteratively from an initial natural language task description. The proposed method extends this mechanism to adapt a refined imitation policy to new target configurations and incorporate constraints via human feedback corrections during intermediate executions. This allows policy reuse and improves data efficiency.

Empirical Benchmarks:
• Baseline Imitation Policy: Generic unrefined policy achieved 21.2% success under out-of-distribution shifts.
• Autonomous Isaac Lab RL: Training in Isaac Lab elevated task convergence to 98.0%.
• PRISM Framework: Combining iterative GPT-5 reward generation with interactive human feedback achieved 96.8% empirical success while dramatically lowering sample complexity.

A Virtual Reality Framework for Rapid Dataset Generation Applied to Garment Manipulation with Automatic Semantic Labeling

Co-Author · IEEE ICRA 2023 [London, UK]
Júlia Borràs, Arnau Boix-Granell, Sergi Foix, Carme Torras
HTC Vive Pro VR Cloth Manipulation Unity 3D Engine Automatic Semantic Labeling C++ 19 Manipulation Sequences

Summary: Teaching complex manipulation skills, such as folding garments, to a dual-arm robot is a very challenging task often addressed through learning from demonstration. The few garment-folding demonstration datasets currently available to the robotics research community have either been gathered from human demonstrations or generated via simulation. The former approach presents major difficulties in perceiving both the garment state and human actions and transferring them to the robot's dynamic control, whereas the latter requires open-loop programming of human motions in the simulator without incorporating the visual feedback that humans naturally use, leading to unrealistic movements.

In this paper, we present an accurate dataset of human garment-folding demonstrations collected through our novel virtual reality (VR) framework based on Unity 3D and the HTC Vive Pro system. This platform enables realistic simulation of deformable textiles and real-time interaction using handheld controllers. By closing the human-in-the-loop perceptual feedback loop while recording ground-truth 3D mesh states without camera occlusions, the framework yields highly realistic demonstrations. The dataset provides automatic semantic labeling classifying key topological folding configurations across 19 distinct garment manipulation sequences.

Garment Manipulation Dataset for Robot Learning from Demonstration Using a Virtual Reality Framework

Lead Author · CCIA 2022 [IOS Press FAIA]
Arnau Boix-Granell, Sergi Foix, Carme Torras
Learning from Demonstration HTC Vive Pro Cloth Manipulation Unity 3D Engine C++ IOS Press Vol. 355

Summary: Teaching complex bimanual manipulation skills to robots requires rich, multi-modal demonstration datasets. In this paper, we present a compact yet highly accurate dataset of human garment-folding trajectories collected using an immersive virtual reality platform. Implemented in Unity 3D with the HTC Vive Pro tracking system, the environment reproduces realistic textile mechanics and enables natural handheld interaction. By bridging the gap between human perception and robotic action spaces, the framework eliminates occlusion artifacts typical of optical motion capture while ensuring physically feasible grasping trajectories for dual-arm robotic systems. Published in Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence and Applications (IOS Press, Vol. 355).
